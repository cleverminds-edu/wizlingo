# PHASE 2 — The 15-Level Mastery Map

5 Groups × 3 Levels = **15 visible mastery levels**.

Naming principle: each group is a **World** with an identity; each level is a **Rank** within it.
Ranks are aspirational and game-like, but every one is a defensible description of a language
capability — so the same word appears on a child's map and on a school's progress report
without embarrassment on either side. The terminal rank, **Language Wizard**, is deliberately
continuous with the existing WizLingo badge line (Word Wizard → Voice Wizard → Language Wizard).

# WORLD 1 — SOUND ISLES
*Pre-Primary · Nursery–UKG · The world where language is still made of noises, and noises turn out to have names.*

---

## Level 1.1 — SOUND SCOUT

- **Ages:** 3–4 · **Grades:** Nursery · **CEFR:** Pre-A1 (emergent)
- **Learning identity:** *"I can hear the difference between sounds."*
- **Overall outcome:** The learner attends to spoken English, discriminates between clearly
  contrasting sounds, and produces single recognisable words in response to prompts.

| Dimension | Expectation |
|---|---|
| **Vocabulary** | 300 receptive / 150 productive. Concrete nouns and 15 high-frequency verbs from immediate environment: body, family, food, animals, toys, colours. |
| **Grammar** | None explicit. Single-word utterances; holophrases ("more milk") accepted as whole units. |
| **Reading** | Pre-reading. Recognises own name in print; tracks left-to-right with a finger; knows a book has a front and a top. Recognises 10–15 uppercase letter *shapes* (not necessarily sounds). |
| **Writing** | Pre-writing. Controlled scribble, vertical/horizontal/circular strokes, traces 6–8 letter forms with a guide. |
| **Listening** | Follows one-step spoken instructions. Discriminates loud/soft, fast/slow, same/different for maximally contrasting sounds. Attends to a 60–90 s read-aloud. |
| **Speaking** | Imitates single words with recognisable initial consonants. Names 40+ pictured objects. Answers "what is this?" |
| **Cognitive** | Matching, sorting by one attribute, 2–3 item memory span. Recognition rather than recall. |
| **Communication** | Responds to greetings, uses "please/thank you" as ritual, indicates need with word + gesture. |
| **Mastery criteria** | Names 40/50 core pictures; isolates initial sound in 8/10 words *by matching* (not producing); follows 9/10 one-step instructions; 12 sessions of evidence across ≥3 weeks. |

## Level 1.2 — WORD SPOTTER

- **Ages:** 4–5 · **Grades:** LKG · **CEFR:** Pre-A1
- **Learning identity:** *"I know that letters make sounds, and sounds make words."*
- **Overall outcome:** The learner maps all 26 letters to primary sounds, hears and produces
  rhyme and initial sounds, and speaks in 2–3 word phrases.

| Dimension | Expectation |
|---|---|
| **Vocabulary** | 600 / 300. Adds prepositions of place, 20 adjectives (size/colour/feeling), 30 action verbs, family/school/community sets. |
| **Grammar** | Implicit only. Plural `-s`, `a/the`, subject + verb ("dog runs"), `is/are` in fixed frames. No metalanguage. |
| **Reading** | 26 letter–sound correspondences (single letters). Blends 2-phoneme words orally. Reads 15–25 sight words. Recognises own and classmates' names. |
| **Writing** | Writes all 26 letters legibly with a model. Writes own name unaided. Copies 3-letter words. Invented spelling with correct initial letter. |
| **Listening** | Two-step instructions. Identifies rhyming pairs (4/5). Claps syllables in 2-syllable words. 3–5 min story attention with recall of 2 events. |
| **Speaking** | 2–3 word phrases. Produces all 26 letter sounds in isolation. Retells a 3-event story with picture support. Asks "what/where" questions. |
| **Cognitive** | Sorts by two attributes. Sequences 3 pictures. 4-item memory span. Beginning "why" reasoning. |
| **Communication** | Turn-takes in a 3-exchange conversation with an adult. Greets, requests, refuses politely. Describes a picture in 2 sentences. |
| **Mastery criteria** | 24/26 letter sounds produced on demand; 8/10 initial-sound isolation *produced*; 4/5 rhyme identification; 20/25 sight words; oral blending of 8/10 two-phoneme words; evidence across ≥4 weeks and ≥3 activity formats. |

## Level 1.3 — STORY SPROUT

- **Ages:** 5–6 · **Grades:** UKG · **CEFR:** Pre-A1 → A1 threshold
- **Learning identity:** *"I can read little words myself, and I can tell you a story."*
- **Overall outcome:** The learner decodes CVC words independently, reads simple sentences,
  writes a sentence, and narrates a 4–5 event story.

| Dimension | Expectation |
|---|---|
| **Vocabulary** | 1,000 / 500. Adds time words, opposites (25 pairs), category labels, feeling words beyond happy/sad, 50 school-task verbs. |
| **Grammar** | Implicit + first labels ("naming word", "doing word"). Present continuous, simple past of 10 irregulars (`went, ate, saw`), pronouns `I/you/he/she/it`, `and` as connector. |
| **Reading** | Decodes CVC and CVCC words unaided. Reads 2–3 sentence texts with 90% accuracy. 50–75 sight words. Answers literal "who/what/where" from a read text. Beginning digraphs `sh, ch, th`. |
| **Writing** | Writes a dictated CVC word. Composes one 3–5 word sentence with a capital and a full stop. Correct letter formation without a model. Labels a drawing. |
| **Listening** | Three-step instructions. Segments 3-phoneme words orally. Deletes initial phoneme ("say *cat* without /k/"). Recalls 4 events and one detail from a 5-min story. |
| **Speaking** | 3–5 word sentences. Narrates 4–5 events in sequence. Uses `because` once. Asks "why" and "how many". Reads own writing aloud. |
| **Cognitive** | Predicts next event. Sorts by function/category. Simple cause-effect. 5-item span. First inference from picture cues. |
| **Communication** | 5-exchange conversation. Introduces self in 3 sentences. Asks a peer a question. Describes a scene with 4 details. |
| **Mastery criteria** | Decodes 18/20 unseen CVC words; reads a 3-sentence text at ≥90% accuracy with literal comprehension 3/4; segments 8/10 three-phoneme words; writes 2 conventional sentences unaided; narrates 4 sequenced events; evidence across ≥5 weeks, ≥4 formats, retention check passed at ≥14 days. |

# WORLD 2 — WORD FORGE
*Foundation · Grades 1–3 · Where sounds are hammered into words, and words into sentences that hold their shape.*

---

## Level 2.1 — PHONICS PATHFINDER

- **Ages:** 6–7 · **Grades:** 1 · **CEFR:** A1.1
- **Learning identity:** *"I can sound out almost any short word."*
- **Overall outcome:** The learner decodes single-syllable words with digraphs and blends,
  reads a 30–60 word passage, and writes 2–3 connected sentences.

| Dimension | Expectation |
|---|---|
| **Vocabulary** | 1,500 / 800. Word families, 30 compound words, 40 adjectives, first synonym/antonym pairs, topic sets (weather, transport, jobs). |
| **Grammar** | Explicit: noun, verb, adjective as named categories. Singular/plural incl. `-es`. Simple present & past. `a/an/the`. Sentence = capital + end mark. Subject–verb agreement in present. |
| **Reading** | All consonant digraphs and initial/final blends. Magic-`e`. 100–150 sight words. 30–60 word passages at 95% accuracy, 40–60 WCPM. Literal comprehension + one simple inference. Retells beginning/middle/end. |
| **Writing** | 2–3 connected sentences on a prompt. Capitals for names and sentence start; full stop and question mark. Spells CVC, CVCe, digraph words correctly; phonetic attempts elsewhere. Simple sentence expansion with an adjective. |
| **Listening** | Full phoneme segmentation and blending of 4-phoneme words. Phoneme substitution. Follows 3-step instructions. Answers 4 questions after a 6–8 min listening text. |
| **Speaking** | 5–7 word sentences. Reads aloud with attention to end punctuation. Describes a process in 3 steps. Recounts a personal event in 4 sentences. |
| **Cognitive** | Compare/contrast two items. Classify into given categories. Predict with justification. Sequence 5 events. |
| **Communication** | Asks and answers to sustain a short exchange. Presents a 30 s "show and tell". Uses greetings/farewells appropriately by listener. |
| **Mastery criteria** | Decodes 27/30 unseen single-syllable words incl. digraphs & blends; 45+ WCPM at ≥95% accuracy; comprehension 4/5 with ≥1 inference; writes 3 connected sentences with 2/3 conventions; ≥5 formats, ≥6 weeks, delayed retention ≥14 days. |

---

## Level 2.2 — WORD BUILDER

- **Ages:** 7–8 · **Grades:** 2 · **CEFR:** A1.2
- **Learning identity:** *"I can build big words from small parts, and my sentences say more."*
- **Overall outcome:** The learner decodes multi-syllable words using syllable division and
  common affixes, reads 60–120 word texts fluently, and writes a coherent 5-sentence paragraph.

| Dimension | Expectation |
|---|---|
| **Vocabulary** | 2,500 / 1,400. Prefixes `un-, re-, dis-, pre-`; suffixes `-ful, -less, -ly, -er, -est, -ing, -ed`. 60 synonym and 40 antonym pairs. Homophones (`there/their`). Contextual word-meaning strategies. |
| **Grammar** | Adverbs, pronouns (subject/object), possessives, conjunctions `and/but/because/so`. Three tenses (simple present/past/future). Comparatives & superlatives. Statement/question/exclamation/command. Commas in lists. |
| **Reading** | Vowel teams, r-controlled vowels, 2–3 syllable division. 200–300 sight words. 60–120 word texts, 70–90 WCPM at 95%+. Main idea + 2 supporting details. Inference from text evidence. Distinguishes fiction/non-fiction. |
| **Writing** | 5-sentence paragraph with a topic sentence. Varies sentence openings. Uses commas in lists and apostrophes for possession. Correct spelling of level word list + regular affixes. Simple narrative with beginning/middle/end. Basic editing against a 3-item checklist. |
| **Listening** | Note-taking by keyword. Distinguishes fact from opinion in speech. Follows 4-step instructions. Answers inferential questions after a 10-min text. Identifies speaker's feeling from tone. |
| **Speaking** | 7–9 word sentences with subordination via `because/so`. Reads aloud with phrasing and expression. Explains a 4-step process. Retells with sequencing markers (`first, next, finally`). 1-minute prepared talk. |
| **Cognitive** | Cause and effect with evidence. Two-attribute classification. Problem/solution identification. Simple analogy (`hot:cold :: big:?`). |
| **Communication** | Sustains a 6-exchange topic-focused conversation. Disagrees politely. Asks clarifying questions. Works in a pair to reach a shared answer. |
| **Mastery criteria** | Decodes 18/20 unseen 2-syllable words; 75+ WCPM at ≥95%; identifies main idea + 2 details in 4/5 texts; writes a paragraph scoring ≥3/4 on coherence and ≥3/4 on conventions; uses 4 affixes productively; ≥5 formats, ≥6 weeks, retention ≥21 days, ≥1 speaking and ≥1 writing evidence. |

## Level 2.3 — SENTENCE SMITH

- **Ages:** 8–9 · **Grades:** 3 · **CEFR:** A2.1
- **Learning identity:** *"I control my sentences — I can make them long, short, or joined."*
- **Overall outcome:** The learner reads 120–200 word texts for meaning, constructs compound
  and early complex sentences deliberately, and writes a structured 2-paragraph piece.

| Dimension | Expectation |
|---|---|
| **Vocabulary** | 3,500 / 2,000. Latin/Greek roots (`port, dict, scrib, tele, graph`). Shades of meaning (`warm/hot/scorching`). Figurative language: simile, basic idiom. Subject-specific vocabulary from school subjects. |
| **Grammar** | Simple/compound/complex sentence distinction. Subordinating conjunctions (`when, although, if, while`). Progressive & perfect aspect informally. Irregular plurals & past tense. Quotation marks for dialogue. Fragment & run-on identification and repair. |
| **Reading** | Fully fluent decoding of multi-syllable words; morphology-driven attack on unknown words. 90–110 WCPM with expression. 120–200 word texts across genres. Summarises. Infers character motive. Identifies author's purpose. Compares two texts on a shared topic. Uses text features (heading, caption, glossary). |
| **Writing** | Two paragraphs with topic sentences and a linking transition. Compound and complex sentences by choice. Dialogue punctuation. Narrative with a problem and resolution; simple informational report with 3 facts. Revises for word choice using a thesaurus-like prompt. |
| **Listening** | Summarises a 12-min spoken text. Identifies main idea + supporting details from speech. Detects sequence and comparison signals. Distinguishes literal from figurative in speech. |
| **Speaking** | 9–12 word sentences with subordinate clauses. Reads aloud with prosody matching punctuation. Delivers a 2-minute talk with 3 points. Retells a story from a different character's view. Uses transitions in speech. |
| **Cognitive** | Multi-step inference. Compares across two sources. Justifies an opinion with two reasons. Distinguishes relevant from irrelevant detail. Analogy with categorical relations. |
| **Communication** | Group discussion with turn-taking and building on others' ideas ("I agree because…"). Interviews a peer with 4 prepared questions. Adjusts formality between friend and teacher. |
| **Mastery criteria** | 100+ WCPM at ≥97%; summarises 4/5 texts capturing main idea without copying; produces complex sentences correctly in 8/10 constructed responses; 2-paragraph piece ≥3/4 on structure, coherence, conventions; identifies and repairs 8/10 run-ons/fragments; ≥6 formats, ≥8 weeks, retention ≥28 days, evidence in all four LSRW modes. |

# WORLD 3 — MEANING REALMS
*Developing · Grades 4–7 · Where words stop being the point and meaning becomes the point.*

---

## Level 3.1 — MEANING MAPPER

- **Ages:** 9–10 · **Grades:** 4–5 · **CEFR:** A2.2
- **Learning identity:** *"I don't just read the words — I work out what the writer means."*
- **Overall outcome:** The learner comprehends 200–350 word texts inferentially, writes a
  structured 3-paragraph piece, and speaks for 3 minutes with organisation.

| Dimension | Expectation |
|---|---|
| **Vocabulary** | 5,000 / 3,000. Morphological analysis as a primary strategy. Nuance and connotation. Multiple-meaning words disambiguated by context. Academic word list tier-2 entry (`observe, describe, compare, summarise`). Idiom bank ~40. |
| **Grammar** | Clause types (independent/dependent). Relative clauses with `who/which/that`. All 12 tense–aspect forms recognised, 8 used. Modals for possibility & obligation. Active/passive recognition. Comma with introductory phrases; apostrophes fully controlled. |
| **Reading** | 110–130 WCPM. 200–350 word multi-paragraph texts, fiction and non-fiction. Inference from combined clues. Distinguishes fact/opinion in print. Identifies theme. Uses text structure (compare/contrast, cause/effect, sequence) to aid comprehension. Skims and scans purposefully. |
| **Writing** | 3-paragraph structure: introduction, body, conclusion. Paragraph unity and a controlling idea. Varied sentence openings and lengths for effect. Descriptive writing with sensory detail. Opinion writing with 2 supported reasons. Revises for clarity in a second draft. |
| **Listening** | Note-taking in a simple two-column format. Summarises a 15-min lecture-style text. Infers speaker attitude. Identifies bias markers. Follows multi-step spoken procedures. |
| **Speaking** | 12–15 word sentences. 3-minute organised talk with an opening and closing. Participates in a structured debate on a familiar topic. Self-corrects pronunciation of multi-syllable words. Uses word stress correctly in known vocabulary. |
| **Cognitive** | Analysis: breaks a text into parts and explains their function. Synthesises two sources. Evaluates an argument's reasons. Identifies an unstated assumption with support. |
| **Communication** | Leads a small-group task. Summarises a group's position. Asks probing follow-up questions. Registers: informal / school-formal chosen deliberately. Written communication: friendly letter, simple email. |
| **Mastery criteria** | Inferential comprehension ≥80% across 5 texts of ≥250 words; 3-paragraph piece ≥3.5/5 on structure and ≥3.5/5 on language; 3-min talk ≥3.5/5 on organisation and fluency; produces relative clauses correctly in 8/10 items; morphological word attack succeeds on 8/10 unseen derived words; ≥6 formats, ≥8 weeks, retention ≥28 days, all four modes. |

## Level 3.2 — STORY WEAVER

- **Ages:** 10–12 · **Grades:** 5–6 · **CEFR:** B1.1
- **Learning identity:** *"I can build a whole piece of writing or a whole talk that holds together."*
- **Overall outcome:** The learner handles 350–500 word texts analytically, produces
  multi-paragraph writing in three genres, and sustains extended discourse.

| Dimension | Expectation |
|---|---|
| **Vocabulary** | 7,000 / 4,200. Word families across parts of speech (`analyse/analysis/analytical`). Register-graded synonyms (`kid/child/juvenile`). Collocation awareness. Academic vocabulary tier-2 core. Etymology as a retention tool. |
| **Grammar** | Full clause manipulation: combining, reducing, embedding. Participial phrases. Conditionals type 1 & 2. Active↔passive transformation by choice. Reported speech. Semicolon and colon. Consistent tense and pronoun reference across a text. |
| **Reading** | 130–150 WCPM; rate adjusted to purpose. 350–500 word texts incl. informational, literary, persuasive. Analyses author's craft (word choice, structure). Traces an argument. Compares treatments of one topic in two texts. Evaluates evidence sufficiency. Reads a simple graph/table alongside text. |
| **Writing** | Narrative with developed character and a controlled arc; expository with 3 supported points; persuasive with a claim, reasons, and a counter-acknowledgement. Cohesive devices across paragraphs. Deliberate sentence variety. Two-draft process with targeted revision. Basic citation of a named source. |
| **Listening** | Structured note-taking from a 20-min text. Distinguishes claim from evidence in speech. Detects persuasive technique. Comprehends unfamiliar accents of English. Infers implied meaning and tone. |
| **Speaking** | 14–18 word sentences with embedded clauses. 4–5 minute presentation with visual support. Debates with rebuttal. Sentence stress and intonation for emphasis and contrast. Storytelling with pacing and voice. |
| **Cognitive** | Sustained analysis across a whole text. Synthesis of 2–3 sources into one position. Evaluation against stated criteria. Identifies logical gaps. Generates original examples. |
| **Communication** | Chairs a discussion. Negotiates a group decision. Adapts register across three audiences. Formal email; structured request. Gives and receives peer feedback constructively. |
| **Mastery criteria** | Analytical comprehension ≥80% on 5 texts ≥400 words; produces 3 genres each ≥3.5/5 on genre-appropriateness and coherence; 4-min presentation ≥3.5/5 on structure, delivery, language; clause-combining accuracy ≥85%; synthesises 2 sources without copying in 3/3 tasks; ≥7 formats, ≥10 weeks, retention ≥35 days, all four modes. |

## Level 3.3 — IDEA ARCHITECT

- **Ages:** 11–13 · **Grades:** 6–7 · **CEFR:** B1.2
- **Learning identity:** *"I design my ideas before I express them."*
- **Overall outcome:** The learner reasons across 500–650 word texts, plans and produces
  argumentative writing, and speaks analytically with evidence.

| Dimension | Expectation |
|---|---|
| **Vocabulary** | 9,000 / 5,500. Abstract and nominalised vocabulary (`significance, implication, tendency`). Precise verbs of analysis (`suggests, implies, undermines, reinforces`). Discipline-specific registers. Connotation used deliberately in own production. |
| **Grammar** | Nominalisation. Complex noun phrases. Hedging and modality for stance (`may, appears to, arguably`). Conditionals all types. Cohesion via reference chains and substitution. Punctuation for effect (dash, parenthesis). Controls sentence rhythm. |
| **Reading** | 150–170 WCPM. 500–650 word texts incl. argumentative and multi-source. Evaluates argument quality: relevance, sufficiency, credibility. Identifies rhetorical technique and its effect. Detects tone shift. Reads across two texts to resolve a discrepancy. Interprets data visualisations with text. |
| **Writing** | Argumentative essay: claim, 3 developed reasons with evidence, counter-argument and rebuttal, conclusion. Analytical paragraph on a text. Summary that preserves the source's proportions. Explicit planning stage (outline). Revision guided by a rubric. Source attribution. |
| **Listening** | Critical listening: separates assertion, evidence, and appeal. Notes an argument's structure while listening. Comprehends academic and news registers. Identifies what a speaker left out. |
| **Speaking** | 16–20 word sentences with controlled subordination. 5-minute analytical presentation. Formal debate with prepared rebuttal and impromptu response. Intonation for stance and concession. Manages Q&A. |
| **Cognitive** | Evaluates competing claims on criteria. Constructs a counter-argument to their own position. Distinguishes correlation from causation in text. Identifies 4+ named fallacies. Transfers a framework to a new domain. |
| **Communication** | Facilitates a debate. Persuades an identified audience with audience-appropriate appeals. Collaborative writing with role division. Semi-formal and formal written registers. Handles disagreement without escalation. |
| **Mastery criteria** | Argument evaluation ≥80% across 5 texts ≥550 words; argumentative essay ≥4/5 on argument quality and ≥3.5/5 on language; identifies 4/5 fallacies in unseen material; 5-min analytical talk ≥4/5 on reasoning; produces hedged/nominalised academic sentences correctly in 8/10 items; ≥7 formats, ≥12 weeks, retention ≥42 days, all four modes. |

# WORLD 4 — EXPRESSION SUMMIT
*Advanced · Grades 8–10 · Where the learner stops asking "is this correct?" and starts asking "is this effective?"*

> **Presentation note:** From Level 4.1 the game layer becomes *achievement-oriented rather than
> playful*: progress analytics, skill radars, mastery percentages, leaderboards by cohort, and
> credential-style badges. No mascots, no cartoon rewards, no confetti. See
> [Phase 7 §7.7](07_GAME_ARCHITECTURE.md#77-age-appropriate-evolution-of-the-game-layer).

---

## Level 4.1 — VOICE CRAFTER

- **Ages:** 13–14 · **Grades:** 8 · **CEFR:** B1.2 → B2.1
- **Learning identity:** *"I have a voice, and I choose how it sounds."*
- **Overall outcome:** The learner controls style and register deliberately, reads 650–800 word
  texts critically, and writes with an identifiable and intentional voice.

| Dimension | Expectation |
|---|---|
| **Vocabulary** | 11,000 / 7,000. Register-sensitive selection across formal/neutral/colloquial. Figurative and connotative precision. Academic word list substantially productive. Discipline vocabulary for 4+ school subjects. |
| **Grammar** | Stylistic syntax: inversion, cleft sentences, fronting for emphasis. Full aspect control incl. perfect progressive. Subjunctive in formal frames. Ellipsis and substitution for economy. Punctuation as a rhetorical tool. Deliberate sentence-length patterning. |
| **Reading** | 170–190 WCPM, rate strategically varied. 650–800 word texts incl. literary prose, opinion journalism, technical explanation. Analyses how form creates meaning. Identifies authorial stance and its construction. Recognises unreliable narration and loaded language. Synthesises 3 sources. |
| **Writing** | Personal/reflective essay with voice; literary-analytical paragraph with quotation and commentary; explanatory writing for a non-expert audience. Controls tone across a whole piece. Deliberate stylistic choice with a stated rationale. Multi-draft with self-assessment against a rubric. |
| **Listening** | Comprehends extended academic and professional speech (25+ min). Evaluates a speaker's credibility. Notes rhetorical strategy in real time. Handles a range of accents and rapid connected speech. |
| **Speaking** | 18–22 word sentences with stylistic variation. 6-minute presentation with deliberate delivery choices. Extemporaneous response with structure. Controls pace, pause, emphasis, and volume. Discourse markers used naturally. |
| **Cognitive** | Metacognitive: explains their own reasoning and revises it. Evaluates a source's reliability. Constructs multi-premise arguments. Recognises framing effects. Reasons from analogy with awareness of its limits. |
| **Communication** | Adapts a single message to three audiences. Professional email, formal request, structured feedback. Chairs a meeting-style discussion with an agenda. Interviewing and being interviewed. Conflict de-escalation in writing. |
| **Mastery criteria** | Critical comprehension ≥80% on 5 texts ≥700 words; produces 3 genres each ≥4/5 on voice/register control; 6-min presentation ≥4/5 on delivery and structure; register-transformation task ≥85% accurate; stylistic syntax produced correctly in 8/10 items; ≥7 formats, ≥12 weeks, retention ≥42 days, all four modes. |

## Level 4.2 — ARGUMENT FORGER

- **Ages:** 14–15 · **Grades:** 9 · **CEFR:** B2.1
- **Learning identity:** *"I build arguments that survive being attacked."*
- **Overall outcome:** The learner constructs and defends rigorous arguments in writing and
  speech, and evaluates 800–950 word texts for reasoning quality.

| Dimension | Expectation |
|---|---|
| **Vocabulary** | 13,500 / 8,500. Argumentative and evaluative lexis (`presupposes, conflates, substantiates, notwithstanding`). Precise qualification and quantification. Terminology of logic and rhetoric. Near-synonym discrimination under pressure. |
| **Grammar** | Concessive and contrastive structures at clause and discourse level. Complex conditionals and counterfactuals. Extended noun phrases with post-modification. Cohesion across a 900-word text. Grammatical metaphor. |
| **Reading** | 190–210 WCPM. 800–950 word argumentative and analytical texts. Reconstructs an argument as premises and conclusion. Assesses evidence type and strength. Detects sophistry and rhetorical manipulation. Reads two opposing texts and adjudicates. Interprets statistical claims critically. |
| **Writing** | Full argumentative essay (600–800 words) with thesis, reasoned development, integrated evidence, sustained counter-argument, and qualified conclusion. Literary analysis with a defensible interpretation. Research-informed writing with consistent citation. Editing for logic as well as language. |
| **Listening** | Reconstructs a spoken argument's structure. Identifies unstated premises. Evaluates debate performance against criteria. Comprehends dense academic lectures with note-taking. |

## 2.3 Placement and movement

**Initial placement** — Diagnostic Assessment (Phase 5 §5.6) proposes a level. Constraints:
- A learner is never placed more than **2 groups below** their grade's typical group; below that
  floor, the system places them at the floor and flags for teacher intervention, because the
  gap indicates a need beyond adaptive content.
- A learner may be placed **above** their grade's typical level without limit.
- Placement lands at difficulty band **D2 (Supported)** of the chosen level, never D1 or D5, so
  the engine has room to move in both directions during the first fortnight.

**Level-up** requires all of:
1. ≥80% of the level's competencies at `Proficient` or above,
2. ≥60% at `Mastered`,
3. no pillar below 70% of the level's competencies at `Proficient` (prevents lopsided promotion),
4. Level Assessment passed (Phase 5 §5.7),
5. current difficulty band at D4 or D5 and stable for ≥5 sessions.

**Level-down** is rare, deliberately hard, and never framed as demotion. It requires a
sustained drop: mean strand mastery below the level's floor for ≥10 consecutive sessions **and**
difficulty band pinned at D1 for ≥8 sessions. The learner sees "we're revisiting some
foundations", never a level number decreasing. Movement below a level's floor within a level is
handled by the difficulty engine instead — which is why level-down is almost never needed.

**Typical dwell time** — 10–16 weeks per level at 15 min/day, 5 days/week. Faster is expected
for learners placed below their true ability during the first two levels after diagnosis.

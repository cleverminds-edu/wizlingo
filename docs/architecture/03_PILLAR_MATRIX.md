# PHASE 3 — The 8-Pillar Progression Matrix

**The rule: no pillar ever disappears.** All eight are present at all fifteen levels. What
changes is complexity, independence, cognitive load, context, and communicative demand.

A pillar that vanishes at some level is a bug in the curriculum, not a design choice. The most
common temptation is to drop SOUND after Grade 3 — WizLingo does not. SOUND becomes prosody,
then articulation, then vocal delivery, then professional speech. Similarly THINKING does not
begin in Grade 6; at Nursery it is sorting and matching.

---

## 3.1 What each pillar owns

| Pillar | Question it answers | Domains |
|---|---|---|
| **SOUND** | Can the learner perceive and produce the sounds of the language? | Phonemic Awareness · Phonics & Decoding · Pronunciation & Articulation · Prosody & Voice |
| **WORD** | Does the learner have the words, and can they get more on their own? | Vocabulary Breadth · Vocabulary Depth (nuance/connotation) · Morphology · Spelling & Orthography |
| **SENTENCE** | Can the learner build and control grammatical structure? | Syntax & Structure · Grammar Accuracy · Sentence Variety & Style · Mechanics & Punctuation |
| **UNDERSTANDING** | Can the learner extract and construct meaning from input? | Listening Comprehension · Reading Comprehension · Inference & Interpretation · Critical Evaluation |
| **EXPRESSION** | Can the learner produce language to convey their own meaning? | Speaking Production · Writing Production · Fluency · Voice & Register |
| **COMMUNICATION** | Can the learner achieve social purposes with another human? | Conversation & Interaction · Presentation & Public Speaking · Pragmatics & Audience · Collaboration |
| **THINKING** | Can the learner reason with and about language? | Reasoning & Logic · Analysis & Synthesis · Creativity & Originality · Metacognition |
| **MASTERY** | Can the learner retain, transfer, and self-direct? | Retention & Automaticity · Transfer to Novel Contexts · Self-Direction · Integration Across Pillars |

**MASTERY is a real pillar, not a scoreboard.** It is the only pillar assessed exclusively on
*delayed and displaced* evidence: retention checks after 14–90 days, and performance in
contexts unlike the one where the skill was taught. Nothing else in the system measures whether
learning survived. It has its own activities (spaced retrieval quests, transfer challenges,
self-assessment calibration tasks, "teach it back" tasks) and its own weight in level-up
decisions — which is what makes WizLingo's mastery claim honest rather than rhetorical.

## 3.2 PILLAR 1 — SOUND

| Level | Focus | Specifics |
|---|---|---|
| 1.1 | Sound awareness | Attends to speech; discriminates loud/soft, same/different; environmental vs. speech sounds; matches initial sounds |
| 1.2 | Letter–sound mapping | All 26 single letter-sound correspondences; rhyme recognition; syllable clapping; oral blending of 2 phonemes |
| 1.3 | Phonemic manipulation | Segments 3 phonemes; initial-phoneme deletion; digraphs `sh/ch/th`; decodes CVC; produces all phonemes clearly |
| 2.1 | Decoding fluency | Consonant digraphs, initial/final blends, magic-`e`; 4-phoneme segmentation; phoneme substitution; clear articulation of blends |
| 2.2 | Complex graphemes | Vowel teams, r-controlled vowels, 2–3 syllable division; syllable stress in familiar words; soft `c/g` |
| 2.3 | Orthographic automaticity | Multi-syllable decoding by morpheme; schwa awareness; prosodic reading matched to punctuation; word-stress patterns |
| 3.1 | Pronunciation refinement | Word stress in derived words (`PHOtograph → phoTOGraphy`); minimal-pair correction of L1 interference; self-monitored articulation |
| 3.2 | Sentence prosody | Sentence stress for emphasis and contrast; rising/falling intonation for question and statement; connected speech (linking) |
| 3.3 | Intonation for meaning | Intonation carrying stance, concession, doubt; pausing for effect; reduction and weak forms; contrastive stress |
| 4.1 | Delivery control | Pace, pause, volume, and emphasis chosen deliberately; clarity under length; vocal variety across a 6-min talk |
| 4.2 | Fluency under load | Maintains articulation and prosody while reasoning aloud, in rebuttal, and under interruption |
| 4.3 | Rhetorical voice | Vocal delivery shaped to rhetorical purpose; rhythm and cadence; parallelism realised prosodically |
| 5.1 | Register in speech | Phonological register-shifting (academic / professional / conversational); accent comprehension and neutrality by choice |
| 5.2 | Professional speech | Precision and authority in high-stakes speech; sustained clarity; negotiation and presentation delivery |
| 5.3 | Articulation mastery | Keynote-standard delivery; distinctive vocal identity; can diagnose and coach another speaker's pronunciation |

**Spiral note:** phonemic awareness (1.1–2.1) is never re-taught after 2.3 — but its *product*,
orthographic mapping, is continuously reused by WORD's morphology domain, and its *skill*,
sound discrimination, is reused by SOUND's pronunciation domain from 3.1 onward. When a Level
4.2 learner mispronounces a multi-syllable word, the engine can and does drop them to a 2.2
syllable-stress micro-skill diagnostic without moving their visible level.

## 3.10 The spiral mechanism

The spiral is not a scheduling convention. It is enforced by three structures in the data model.

### (a) Concept ≠ Micro Skill

One **Concept** spans many levels; each level realises it as a different **Micro Skill**. The
concept `lex.synonymy` exists once; it has 14 micro skills spread across levels 1.3 → 5.3. This
is what makes "revisit at greater complexity" a queryable fact rather than an aspiration.

```
Concept: lex.synonymy
├── 1.3  ms.syn.another-word-for        (oral, picture-supported)
├── 2.1  ms.syn.identify-pairs          (explicit pairs, high-frequency)
├── 2.2  ms.syn.select-in-context       (choose the fitting synonym)
├── 2.3  ms.syn.shades-of-meaning       (warm/hot/scorching gradient)
├── 3.1  ms.syn.connotation             (positive/negative loading)
├── 3.2  ms.syn.register-grading        (kid/child/juvenile)
├── 3.3  ms.syn.precision-in-argument   (suggests vs. proves)
├── 4.1  ms.syn.stylistic-selection     (synonym chosen for voice)
├── 4.2  ms.syn.evaluative-loading      (near-synonym as argumentative move)
├── 4.3  ms.syn.rhetorical-substitution (euphemism, dysphemism)
├── 5.1  ms.syn.ideological-choice      (lexical choice reveals stance)
├── 5.2  ms.syn.audience-calibration    (plain vs. specialist equivalents)
└── 5.3  ms.syn.teaches-synonymy        (explains nuance to another)
```

### (b) Prerequisite DAG with two edge types

- **HARD prerequisite** — the later micro skill is not scheduled until the earlier reaches
  `Proficient`. Used where the dependency is genuinely blocking (cannot decode CVC without
  letter–sound knowledge).
- **SOFT prerequisite** — the later micro skill may be scheduled, but the engine lowers the
  difficulty band and inserts scaffolding if the earlier is below `Proficient`. Used for
  enabling-but-not-blocking relationships (connotation is easier if you know synonyms, but not
  impossible).

Cycles are rejected at authoring time. The DAG is validated on every curriculum publish.

### (c) Mandatory re-encounter scheduling

Every micro skill that reaches `Mastered` is enrolled in a **retention schedule** at expanding
intervals (7 → 14 → 30 → 60 → 120 days), and every concept carries a
`spiralReencounterLevels[]` list. The Session Planner is *obliged* to satisfy the retention
queue before introducing new material — a session cannot be composed of 100% new content when
retention items are due. This is the concrete implementation of "do not teach once and forget":
it is a scheduler constraint, not a guideline. See
[Phase 6 §6.5](06_AI_ADAPTATION.md#65-the-session-planner).

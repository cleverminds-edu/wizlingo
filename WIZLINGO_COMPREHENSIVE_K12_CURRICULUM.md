## Part 1: Foundational Framework

### 1.1 The Science of Reading Foundation

The WizLingo curriculum is built on the "Five Components of Reading" (National Reading Panel, 2000; supported by 40+ years of research):

#### Component 1: Phonological Awareness (Pre-K to Grade 1)

**Definition:** Understanding that words are made of sounds

**Progression Sequence:**
```
Level 0-1: Rhyme Detection (Ages 3-4)
├─ Recognize rhyming words (cat/bat, not/lot)
├─ Generate rhymes (What rhymes with cat?)
└─ Rhyme games and songs

Level 2-3: Syllable Awareness (Ages 4-5)
├─ Clap syllables in words (el-e-phant = 3 claps)
├─ Count syllables
├─ Blend syllables to make words
└─ Delete syllables

Level 4: Onset-Rime (Ages 5-6)
├─ Identify beginning sounds (onset: /c/ in cat)
├─ Identify ending patterns (rime: /æt/ in cat, bat, rat)
├─ Blend onset + rime (c + at = cat)
└─ Generate words with same rime

Level 5-6: Phoneme Awareness (Ages 6-7)
├─ Isolate sounds in words (/c/ /æ/ /t/ in cat)
├─ Identify sounds in different positions
├─ Blend phonemes into words
├─ Segment words into phonemes
└─ Manipulate phonemes (delete, substitute, add)

WHY THIS MATTERS:
├─ Phonological awareness is THE strongest predictor of reading success
├─ Children who lack phonological awareness struggle with phonics
├─ Must be taught explicitly and systematically
└─ Foundation for all phonics instruction
```

**Teaching Methodology:**
- **Oral first:** All activities are auditory (no letter names initially)
- **Playful:** Games, songs, chants, movement
- **Multi-sensory:** Sound, rhythm, movement, visual
- **Explicit:** Teacher models, then scaffolds practice
- **Systematic:** Clear sequence from simple to complex

**Key Research:**
- Phonological awareness accounts for 50-70% of variance in early reading
- Deficits in phonological awareness strongly predict reading disabilities
- Early intervention (Pre-K to Grade 1) is most effective
- Transfer from awareness activities to reading happens with explicit phonics instruction

#### Component 2: Phonics (Grades K-2, Review in 3-5)

**Definition:** Understanding sound-symbol relationships and using these to decode words

**WizLingo Phonics Framework: Jolly Phonics + Science of Reading Integration**

```
PHASE 1: Letter Sound Introduction (K-1, Weeks 1-15)
├─ Introduce 42 letter sounds (42 graphemes for 44 English phonemes)
├─ Sequence: high-frequency + consistent + useful
├─ Order of introduction (adapted Jolly Phonics):
│
│  Week 1-2: s, a, t, p (easy to blend: sat, pat, tap, at)
│  Week 3-4: i, n, m, d (allows more words: man, sit, mid, nit)
│  Week 5-6: g, o, c, k (review blending: got, dog, cog, cod)
│  Week 7-8: e, u, r, h (more complex: red, run, her, hug)
│  Week 9-10: b, f, l, j (word building: lab, fab, fill, jab)
│  Week 11-12: v, w, x, y, z (complete basic set)
│  Week 13-14: Review & consolidate all 18 letters
│  Week 15: Introduction to letter formation (handwriting begins)
│
├─ Teaching Method (Multi-Sensory):
│  ├─ Sound model (hear correct pronunciation)
│  ├─ Letter formation (trace, write)
│  ├─ Word association (s = snake, snake sound /s/)
│  ├─ Action (gross motor movement for each sound)
│  └─ Practice (dozens of examples)
│
└─ Why This Order:
   ├─ Consonants before vowels (easier to isolate)
   ├─ High-frequency sounds first
   ├─ Allows for early CVC (consonant-vowel-consonant) word reading
   └─ Builds confidence and early success

PHASE 2: Simple Blending (K-1, Weeks 16-25)
├─ Blend individual sounds into words
├─ Pattern: CVC words (consonant-vowel-consonant)
│  ├─ Examples: cat, sit, dog, run, bed, hot
│  ├─ Sequence: Start with 3 sounds → 4 sounds → 5 sounds
│  └─ Decodable practice: Only use sounds already taught
│
├─ Blending Technique (Elkonin boxes):
│  ├─ Write sounds in boxes: |c|a|t|
│  ├─ Point and say each sound
│  ├─ Sweep finger under boxes while blending: caaattt → cat
│  └─ Repeat with multiple words
│
└─ Early Reading Materials:
   ├─ Decodable readers (95%+ decodable words)
   ├─ Short sentences (4-6 words)
   ├─ High-frequency words (the, a, and) taught separately
   └─ Illustrations support meaning

PHASE 3: Digraphs & Blends (1-2)
├─ Digraphs (2 letters = 1 sound):
│  ├─ Consonant digraphs: ch, sh, th, ng, ph, wh
│  ├─ Vowel digraphs: ea (eat), oa (boat), ai (rain), ay (play)
│  └─ Pattern: Teach sound first, then words using that sound
│
├─ Consonant Blends (2-3 letters = 2-3 sounds):
│  ├─ Initial blends: bl, br, cl, cr, dr, fl, fr, gr, pr, sk, sl, sm, sn, sp, st, str, sw, tr, tw
│  ├─ Final blends: nd, ng, nk, nt, ld, st
│  └─ Practice words: stop, spring, blend, stand
│
└─ Sequence:
   ├─ Teach easiest digraphs first (ch, sh, th)
   ├─ Then move to vowel digraphs
   ├─ Then consonant blends
   └─ Cumulative: always include previously learned sounds

PHASE 4: Complex Phonics (2-3)
├─ Vowel Control:
│  ├─ Short vowel + consonant: cat, sit, dog
│  ├─ Long vowel (silent e rule): cake, site, dome
│  ├─ Vowel team patterns: boat, train, tree, joy
│  └─ R-controlled vowels: car, for, her, sir
│
├─ Syllable Types (6 types):
│  ├─ Closed: cat, sit, dog (ends in consonant, short vowel)
│  ├─ Open: no, go, be (ends in vowel, long vowel sound)
│  ├─ Silent e: cake, note, smile (vowel-consonant-e, long vowel)
│  ├─ Vowel team: boat, rain, tree (two vowels make one sound)
│  ├─ Consonant-le: table, apple, little (syllable ends in le)
│  └─ R-controlled: car, for, her (vowel + r changes sound)
│
├─ Morphology Introduction:
│  ├─ Root words: play, walk, look
│  ├─ Prefixes: un-, re-, pre- (add to root: unhappy, replay)
│  ├─ Suffixes: -ing, -ed, -er, -ly (add to root: playing, walked)
│  └─ Compound words: sunshine, rainbow, playground
│
└─ Fluency Development:
   ├─ Sight words (high-frequency, irregular): the, was, said, could
   ├─ Automaticity building: timed reading, repeated reading
   ├─ Expression: Read like talking, notice punctuation
   └─ Speed benchmark: Grade 1 end = 60 wpm, Grade 2 end = 100 wpm

PHASE 5: Advanced Phonics (3-5)
├─ Prefixes & Suffixes (expanded):
│  ├─ Common prefixes: un-, re-, pre-, dis-, mis-, over-, under-
│  ├─ Common suffixes: -tion, -sion, -ness, -ment, -able, -ible, -ful
│  └─ Meaning patterns: un-happy = not happy, re-play = play again
│
├─ Word Families & Patterns:
│  ├─ Pattern families: -ould (would, could, should)
│  ├─ Greek/Latin roots: photo (light), graph (write), bio (life)
│  └─ Recognition: Many words follow same patterns
│
├─ Syllabication Rules:
│  ├─ VCV pattern (vowel-consonant-vowel): divide after first V (ba-ker)
│  ├─ VCCV pattern (vowel-consonant-consonant-vowel): divide between C's (nap-kin)
│  └─ Helps with multisyllabic word decoding
│
└─ Spelling Patterns:
   ├─ Words follow patterns: -tion, -sion, -ough
   ├─ Learn to recognize patterns when reading
   └─ Apply patterns when spelling

PHASE 6: Advanced Reading (5-12)
├─ Focus shifts from decoding to comprehension
├─ Phonics still important for unfamiliar words
├─ Vocabulary expansion becomes primary
├─ Morphology understanding accelerates (Greek/Latin roots)
└─ Students can tackle most words independently

CRITICAL PRINCIPLE - Systematic Phonics Instruction:
✓ Explicit: Teacher explains sound-symbol relationships directly
✓ Systematic: Follows logical sequence (simple → complex)
✓ Cumulative: Each lesson builds on previous learning
✓ Decodable Practice: 95%+ of words students can decode
✗ NOT incidental or exploratory
✗ NOT guessing from pictures or context
✗ NOT limited to a few weeks—sustained through Grade 2, review in 3-5
```

**Why This Matters:**
- Phonics instruction accounts for 30-40% of variance in reading ability
- WITHOUT explicit phonics, many children don't learn to decode efficiently
- Effect size is large (0.97) across hundreds of studies
- Children with dyslexia/reading difficulties need MORE phonics, not less

#### Component 3: Fluency (Grades 1-5, Ongoing)

**Definition:** Reading automatically and with expression, connecting meaning

**Fluency Development:**

```
AUTOMATICITY (Automatic word recognition)
├─ Goal: Recognize high-frequency words without thinking
├─ Method: Repeated exposure + practice
├─ Benchmark:
│  ├─ Grade 1: 60 wpm with 90% accuracy
│  ├─ Grade 2: 100 wpm with 95% accuracy
│  ├─ Grade 3: 120 wpm with 95% accuracy
│  └─ Grade 5+: 140+ wpm with 95% accuracy
│
└─ Teaching Techniques:
   ├─ Sight word instruction (high-frequency words)
   ├─ Repeated reading of same texts
   ├─ Timed reading with feedback
   ├─ Chunking (reading in phrases)
   └─ Fluency passages (controlled vocabulary, increasing length)

PROSODY (Reading with expression and intonation)
├─ Meaning-making: Pause at commas, emphasize important words
├─ Punctuation awareness: Question mark = rising tone
├─ Character voices: Dialog = different voices
├─ Phrasing: Read words in meaningful chunks
│
└─ Teaching:
   ├─ Model fluent reading (teacher reads aloud)
   ├─ Echo reading (teacher reads line, students repeat)
   ├─ Choral reading (all read together)
   ├─ Partner reading (pairs read dialogue)
   └─ Independent reading (student reads aloud with feedback)

COMPREHENSION MONITORING
├─ Self-correction: "That doesn't make sense, let me reread"
├─ Adjusting speed: Read slowly for hard parts, faster for easy
├─ Asking questions: "What does this word mean?"
└─ Checking understanding: "Am I understanding this story?"
```

#### Component 4: Vocabulary (K-12, Ongoing)

**Definition:** Knowing the meaning of words and how to use them

**Vocabulary Research Foundation:**

```
THE VOCABULARY GAP (Hart & Risley, 1995; Updated Research):
├─ By age 3, high-SES children: 900+ words, 1500 parents utterances/hour
├─ By age 3, low-SES children: 500 words, 600 parent utterances/hour
├─ This gap increases over time (Matthew Effect)
├─ Academic vocabulary (school language) is often NOT learned at home
└─ IMPLICATION: Schools MUST teach vocabulary explicitly

THREE-TIER VOCABULARY MODEL (Beck, McKeown, Kucan):

TIER 1: Conversational Vocabulary (Basic words)
├─ Known by most children before school
├─ Examples: table, run, happy, big, mother
├─ Typically NOT taught in school (children know them)
├─ Frequency: Very high
└─ Teaching: Only for ELL or language-delayed students

TIER 2: Academic/Instruction Vocabulary (High-utility, cross-curricular)
├─ Necessary for academic success across subjects
├─ Examples: analyze, determine, procedure, conclude, compare
├─ HIGH IMPACT on reading comprehension & academic achievement
├─ Frequency: Medium (appears across texts and subjects)
├─ Teaching: EXPLICIT, with multiple exposures, in context
└─ Estimate: 6,000-7,000 words in English; teach 200-300/year

TIER 3: Specialized/Domain Vocabulary (Subject-specific)
├─ Technical terms used in specific fields
├─ Examples: photosynthesis, mitochondria, democracy, onomatopoeia
├─ Frequency: Low (specific to content area)
├─ Teaching: In context of content being studied
└─ Students learn as needed for specific units

VOCABULARY INSTRUCTION PRINCIPLES:
✓ EXPLICIT: Teacher directly teaches words, doesn't assume knowledge
✓ CONTEXTUAL: Teach words in meaningful context (not word lists)
✓ MULTIPLE EXPOSURES: Need 5-17 exposures for retention
✓ ELABORATED RELATIONSHIPS: Connect to other words (word families)
✓ ATTENTION FOCUSING: Highlight new words, mark them
✓ PRODUCTION: Students use words in speaking & writing
✓ REPETITION: Spaced repetition with increasing difficulty
✓ VARIED CONTEXTS: Same word used in different texts/situations

VOCABULARY GRADE-LEVEL TARGETS:
├─ PreK: 2,500-4,000 words (receptive)
├─ K: 5,000-6,000 words (receptive), 1,500-2,500 (expressive)
├─ Grade 1: 8,000-10,000 words
├─ Grade 2: 12,000-14,000 words
├─ Grade 3: 16,000-20,000 words
├─ Grade 4: 20,000-24,000 words
├─ Grade 5+: 20,000-40,000 words (rapid growth)
└─ College: 50,000+ words (comprehension)

NOTE: These numbers represent CUMULATIVE vocabulary.
Typically 2,000-3,000 NEW words per grade level.
```

#### Component 5: Comprehension (K-12)

**Definition:** Understanding what you read and thinking about it

**Comprehension Framework:**

```
BEFORE READING (Activation & Prediction)
├─ Activate prior knowledge: "What do you know about families?"
├─ Preview text: Look at pictures, read title, predict what will happen
├─ Set purpose: "We're reading to find out..."
├─ Predict: "What do you think will happen?"
└─ WHY: Links new information to existing knowledge; creates interest

DURING READING (Active Comprehension Monitoring)
├─ Visualization: Create pictures in mind ("I can see the dog running")
├─ Connection-making: Connect to personal experience or other texts
├─ Questioning: Ask yourself questions while reading
├─ Prediction confirmation/revision: Were your predictions right?
├─ Clarification: When confused, reread or ask for help
└─ WHY: Keeps reader engaged; helps catch confusion immediately

AFTER READING (Integration & Extension)
├─ Recall: What happened in the story?
├─ Summarization: Condense key ideas into 2-3 sentences
├─ Question answering: Answer comprehension questions
├─ Inferential thinking: Read between lines ("Why did character do that?")
├─ Critical thinking: Evaluate ("Was the character's decision good?")
├─ Extension: Connection to other texts, real world, personal experience
└─ WHY: Consolidates learning; deepens understanding

COMPREHENSION STRATEGIES (Teach Explicitly):
├─ Prediction: Use clues to guess what will happen
├─ Clarification: Reread confusing parts, break into chunks
├─ Questioning: Ask questions while reading
├─ Connection: Link to prior knowledge, other texts
├─ Summarization: Condense main ideas
├─ Visualization: Make pictures in your mind
├─ Inferring: Read between lines; use clues to figure out implicit info
└─ Synthesizing: Put ideas together to form new understanding

QUESTION TYPES (Bloom's Taxonomy-Aligned):
├─ LITERAL (Recall): "What is the boy's name?" (information in text)
├─ INFERENTIAL (Interpret): "Why was he sad?" (between the lines)
├─ EVALUATIVE (Analyze): "Was his decision good?" (judgments)
├─ CREATIVE (Synthesize): "What would you have done?" (connections)
└─ CRITICAL (Evaluate): "Do you agree with the author?" (evaluation)

TEXT STRUCTURE AWARENESS (Grade 2+):
├─ Narrative: Beginning-middle-end, character, setting, problem, solution
├─ Descriptive: Describes something using details
├─ Sequence: Steps or events in order
├─ Compare-contrast: How things are similar/different
├─ Cause-effect: Why something happened (cause) and what resulted (effect)
└─ Problem-solution: Problem is introduced, solution is found

METACOGNITION (Thinking About Thinking):
├─ Monitoring comprehension: "Do I understand this?"
├─ Knowing what to do when confused: "I'll reread that part"
├─ Adjusting strategy: "This is hard, I'll read slower"
├─ Evaluating effectiveness: "Did that strategy help?"
└─ WHY: Essential for independent learning; prevents passive reading
```

**Comprehension Difficulty Progression:**

```
GRADE K-1: Picture Books, Predictable Books

├─ Comprehensible vocabulary & simple grammar

GRADE 4-5: Chapter Books, Content Area Texts

GRADE 6-8: Young Adult Literature, Textbooks
├─ Sophisticated narratives, poetry, drama, informational text

GRADE 9-12: Classic Literature, Academic Texts
├─ Complex literature, academic writing, research texts
├─ Advanced vocabulary & complex sentence structures

└─ Focus: Literary analysis, argumentation, academic communication
```

### 1.2 CEFR Standards Integration

**European Framework for Languages (CEFR) Alignment:**

```
CEFR LEVEL PROGRESSION WITH AGE-GRADE EQUIVALENTS:

Pre-A1 (Beginner Foundation) - Ages 3-5 (Pre-K to K)
├─ Listening: Recognize sounds, familiar words, simple greetings
├─ Speaking: Produce single words, very simple phrases (with support)
├─ Reading: Recognize letters, familiar words, very short text
├─ Writing: Copy letters, simple labels (with support)
├─ Vocabulary: 500-800 words (receptive)
├─ Grammar: Single words, 2-word phrases
└─ Characteristics: Pre-literacy skills, phonological awareness, letter recognition

A1 (Elementary) - Ages 6-9 (Grades 1-3)
├─ Listening: Understand basic greetings, short messages on familiar topics
├─ Speaking: Ask/answer simple questions, introduce self, use familiar phrases
├─ Reading: Understand simple sentences about familiar topics
├─ Writing: Write simple sentences with support, fill in forms
├─ Vocabulary: 1,000-1,500 words
├─ Grammar: Present tense, basic questions, simple negatives
├─ Characteristics: Beginning reader/writer, conversational phrases, simple texts

A2 (Elementary Upper) - Ages 10-13 (Grades 4-6)
├─ Listening: Understand main points, clear speech about familiar topics
├─ Speaking: Communicate about familiar topics, describe experiences
├─ Reading: Understand texts about familiar topics (stories, simple articles)
├─ Writing: Write short paragraphs, email, simple narratives
├─ Vocabulary: 2,000-2,500 words
├─ Grammar: Past tense, future tense, conditional structures
├─ Characteristics: Fluent reader, expanding writer, more complex ideas

B1 (Intermediate) - Ages 14-16 (Grades 8-10)
├─ Listening: Understand main points of clear speech, main ideas of broadcasts
├─ Speaking: Participate in discussions, express opinions, describe complex topics
├─ Reading: Understand articles, stories with unfamiliar vocabulary (from context)
├─ Writing: Write paragraphs about topics of interest, personal letters
├─ Vocabulary: 3,500-4,000 words
├─ Grammar: Complex sentences, passive voice, subjunctive mood, varied tenses
├─ Characteristics: Independent reader/writer, critical thinking, complex discourse

B2 (Upper-Intermediate) - Ages 17-18 (Grades 11-12)
├─ Listening: Understand most TV, speeches, lectures with unfamiliar topics
├─ Speaking: Speak fluently, participate in debates, express subtle nuances
├─ Reading: Read literature, technical articles, understand complex writing
├─ Writing: Write essays, complex arguments, varied text types
├─ Vocabulary: 4,000-5,000+ words
├─ Grammar: Near-native mastery, idiomatic expressions, literary language
└─ Characteristics: Advanced learner, can engage with complex texts and ideas

BENCHMARKS:
├─ A1 → A2: ~300 hours of instruction
├─ A2 → B1: ~600 additional hours
├─ B1 → B2: ~900 additional hours
└─ K-12 (13 years): 1,000-1,500 hours of instruction average
   (200+ hours/year in intensive programs like WizLingo)
```

## Part 3: Detailed Curriculum Components by Grade Band

### 3.1 PREPRIMARY (Ages 3-5) - Levels 1-3

**Developmental Characteristics:**
- Short attention span (5-10 minutes optimal)
- Learning through play and movement
- Egocentric worldview
- Developing gross motor skills
- Language explosion phase
- Imagination and fantasy integral to learning

**Learning Approach: Multi-Sensory Play-Based**

```
TYPICAL WEEK STRUCTURE (15 hours/week):
└─ Sessions: 5 × 45 minutes (song/movement + learning + play)
   
   Session 1: Sound Play & Phonological Awareness
   ├─ Warm-up: Movement song (10 min)
   ├─ Main: Rhyming games, sound discrimination (20 min)
   ├─ Practice: Rhythm activities, clapping syllables (10 min)
   └─ Close: Sing-along (5 min)

   Session 2: Letter Recognition & Tracing
   ├─ Warm-up: Letter dance (10 min)
   ├─ Main: Letter shape, sound, action (20 min)
   ├─ Practice: Sand tracing, playdough letters (10 min)
   └─ Close: Picture book (5 min)

   Session 3: Vocabulary & Listening Stories
   ├─ Warm-up: Movement game (10 min)
   ├─ Main: Story with props/actions (20 min)
   ├─ Practice: Story comprehension game (10 min)
   └─ Close: Sing-along (5 min)

   Session 4: Speaking & Interaction
   ├─ Warm-up: Call-response song (10 min)
   ├─ Main: Role play, puppet conversations (20 min)
   ├─ Practice: Partner activity, take turns speaking (10 min)
   └─ Close: Group sharing (5 min)

   Session 5: Integration & Creative Play
   ├─ Warm-up: Free play (10 min)
   ├─ Main: Project, craft, or creative activity (20 min)
   ├─ Practice: Application of learning (10 min)
   └─ Close: Circle time reflection (5 min)

CONTENT FOCUS:

Level 1 Content (12 weeks):
├─ Rhyming (cat/bat, dog/log, etc.)
├─ Syllable awareness (clapping, counting)
├─ Beginning sounds (s, m, t, p)
├─ Colors (primary: red, yellow, blue)
├─ Numbers 1-5
├─ Family members (mom, dad, baby)
├─ Common animals (dog, cat, bird)
├─ Basic actions (run, jump, sit, sleep)
└─ Emotions (happy, sad, sleepy)

Level 2 Content (16 weeks):
├─ Letters A-M (uppercase, shape & sound)
├─ Phoneme awareness (initial sounds)
├─ Colors (expansion: green, orange, purple)
├─ Numbers 1-10
├─ Body parts (head, hands, feet, eyes)
├─ Foods (apple, milk, bread, banana)
├─ Clothing items (shirt, shoes, hat)
├─ Prepositions intro (in, on, under)
└─ Weather (sunny, rainy, cloudy)

Level 3 Content (16 weeks):
├─ Letters N-Z (uppercase & lowercase beginning)
├─ Letter-sound automaticity
├─ Simple blending (consonant + vowel: ma, ta, ca)
├─ Sight words intro (the, a, is, and)
├─ Shapes (circle, square, triangle)
├─ Seasons
├─ Community helpers (teacher, doctor)
├─ Transportation (car, bus, train)
└─ Music & instruments
```

**Assessment (Preprimary):**
- Phonological awareness (rhyme recognition, syllable counting)

- Letter-sound correspondence (20/26 correct)
- Receptive vocabulary (can point to 300+ picture-named words)
- Expressive language (speak 2-3 word phrases)
- Fine motor skills (pencil grip, basic mark-making)

**Key Resources:**
- Picture cards with sounds/letters
- Manipulatives (letter tiles, counters for syllables)
- Songs & rhymes (nursery rhymes, alphabet songs)
- Decodable picture books
- Movement activities
- Sand/playdough for letter formation

```
├─ Irony (opposite of what's expected)
├─ Pun/Wordplay (jokes with multiple meanings)
└─ Allusion (reference to other works/people)

Tone & Mood:
├─ Tone: Author's attitude toward subject
├─ Mood: Feeling created for reader
├─ Word choice creating tone/mood
├─ Syntax (sentence structure) affecting tone
└─ How tone/mood changes throughout

Author's Craft:
├─ Style (writer's unique way of writing)
```

## Part 7: Special Populations & Differentiation

### Support for Struggling Readers

```
CHARACTERISTICS OF STRUGGLING READERS:
├─ Weak phonological awareness (difficulty with rhyme, syllables)
├─ Difficulty with phonics (slow decoding, many errors)
├─ Low fluency (reading slowly, word-by-word)
├─ Poor comprehension (struggle to understand even decodable text)
├─ Limited vocabulary (fewer words than peers)
├─ Written expression difficulties
└─ Possible indicators of dyslexia

INTERVENTIONS:

Intensive Phonics:
├─ Explicit, systematic, multisensory phonics
├─ More practice with same sounds
├─ Slower pace, smaller steps
├─ Repeated exposure to same words/patterns
├─ Hand-on manipulatives (letter tiles, sandpaper letters)
├─ Kinesthetic input (tracing, writing, movement)
└─ Small group or individual instruction

Fluency Building:
├─ Repeated reading (same text multiple times)
├─ Paired reading (read with partner)
├─ Echo reading (teacher reads line, student repeats)
├─ Choral reading (read together)
├─ Timed readings with feedback
├─ Fluency phrases practice
└─ Motivating texts with high interest

Comprehension Support:
├─ Pre-reading (activate background knowledge, preview words)
├─ During-reading (pause, ask questions, check understanding)
├─ Post-reading (discuss, answer questions, retell)
├─ Graphic organizers (story maps, concept maps)
├─ Visual supports (pictures, illustrations, videos)
├─ Simplified texts at lower levels
└─ Teacher-led small group reading

Vocabulary Instruction:
├─ Pre-teach vocabulary before reading
├─ Use visuals and real objects
├─ Multiple exposures across texts
├─ Connect to background knowledge
├─ High-frequency word focus
└─ Semantic mapping (word families, categories)

Writing Support:
├─ Sentence starters and frames
├─ Word banks
├─ Graphic organizers for planning
├─ Scribed writing (adult writes what student dictates)
├─ Dictation software
├─ Simple formats (labels, lists before full sentences)
└─ Heavy scaffolding and gradual release

DOSAGE & INTENSITY:
├─ 30-45 minutes daily intervention (in addition to core instruction)
├─ Small group (2-4 students) or 1:1
├─ 3-5 days per week minimum
├─ Progress monitoring every 1-2 weeks
├─ Adjust intensity based on progress
└─ Continue for 12+ weeks minimum before evaluating

COLLABORATION:
├─ Classroom teacher + specialist (reading specialist, SLP, special ed)
├─ Communication and consistency
├─ Shared data on progress
├─ Regular meetings to adjust plan
└─ Family involvement in home reading activities
```

### Support for English Language Learners (ELL)

```
LANGUAGE DEVELOPMENT STAGES:

Stage 1: Silent Period (0-6 months)
├─ Listening and comprehending
├─ Very little or no oral production
├─ Building vocabulary receptively
├─ Non-verbal communication
└─ Teaching: Use visuals, gestures, simple language

Stage 2: Early Production (6 months - 1 year)
├─ Speaking 1-2 word phrases
├─ Understanding simple questions
├─ Expanding vocabulary
├─ Pronunciation developing
└─ Teaching: Modeling, controlled practice, lots of repetition

Stage 3: Speech Emergence (1-3 years)
├─ Speaking in simple sentences
├─ Asking and answering questions
├─ Understanding longer passages
├─ Accent still present
└─ Teaching: Scaffolding, guided practice, peer interaction

Stage 4: Intermediate Fluency (3-5 years)
├─ Speaking in complex sentences
├─ Understanding grade-level content
├─ Using academic vocabulary
├─ Grammar mostly correct
└─ Teaching: Grade-level content with support, focus on academic language

Stage 5: Advanced Fluency (5+ years)
├─ Near-native proficiency
├─ Grade-level reading and writing
├─ Nuanced understanding
├─ Minor grammar errors only
└─ Teaching: Grade-level curriculum with enrichment

SPECIFIC SUPPORTS:

Vocabulary:
├─ Pre-teach high-frequency words before lessons
├─ Use visuals, real objects, demonstrations
├─ Sentence frames for structured language practice
├─ Word walls and vocabulary displays
├─ L1 (native language) support when possible
├─ Cognate identification (similar words in L1/English)
└─ Multiple exposures across contexts

Reading Support:
├─ Simplified text or translated text
├─ Audio support (read-alongs, audiobooks)
├─ Picture books and visual supports
├─ Paired reading with fluent English speaker
├─ Vocabulary pre-teaching
├─ Graphic organizers
└─ Comprehension focus over fluency

Writing Support:
├─ Models and examples of target writing
├─ Sentence starters and frames
├─ Word banks and vocabulary lists
├─ Graphic organizers for planning
├─ Peer feedback in supportive environment
├─ Teacher conferencing and guidance
└─ Acceptance of errors as part of learning

Speaking Support:
├─ Sentence frames for structured output
├─ Small group discussions before whole class
├─ Partner practice before sharing with group
├─ Modeling by fluent speakers
├─ Lots of rehearsal time
├─ Acceptance of different accents and simple speech
└─ Positive reinforcement

Grammar Support:
├─ Explicit grammar instruction tied to language level
├─ Focus on high-frequency structures
├─ Teach in meaningful contexts
├─ Model correct form (not error correction)
├─ Multiple exposures and practice
└─ Error correction minimal and supportive

ASSESSMENT CONSIDERATIONS:
├─ Assess content knowledge separately from language proficiency
├─ Use visuals and demonstrations in assessments
├─ Provide extra time for processing
├─ Accept responses in L1 when appropriate
├─ Use performance-based assessments
├─ Monitor progress in language development
├─ Compare to other ELLs, not native English speakers
└─ Include informal observations of language use
```

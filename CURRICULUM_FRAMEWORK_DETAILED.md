## Curriculum Philosophy & Principles

### Core Pedagogical Approach

**Communicative Language Teaching (CLT)**
- Focus on meaningful communication over grammar drills
- Tasks and activities reflect real-world language use
- Student-centered learning with teacher as facilitator
- Integration of all four skills (LSRW)

**Thematic Organization**
- Content organized around meaningful, age-appropriate themes
- Themes are culturally relevant to Indian context
- Each theme builds vocabulary and language functions
- Themes spiral: revisited at increasing complexity levels

**Scaffolded Learning**
- Clear progression from simple to complex
- Support structures gradually removed as competence increases
- Modeling, guided practice, then independent practice
- Multiple exposures to key vocabulary and grammar

**Skill Integration**
- LSRW skills taught in integrated manner, not isolation
- A single theme may have 5 different content pieces (one per skill)
- Skills reinforce each other
- Assessment measures all four skills

**Culturally Responsive Teaching**
- Content reflects Indian cultural context
- Includes local examples, festivals, and traditions
- Acknowledges linguistic diversity
- Builds on students' background knowledge

### Learning Theory Foundation

**Based On:**
- **Krashen's Input Hypothesis**: Comprehensible input is essential
- **Vygotsky's Zone of Proximal Development**: Scaffolding crucial for learning
- **Swain's Output Hypothesis**: Producing language essential for acquisition
- **Bloom's Taxonomy**: Progression from Remember → Apply → Create
- **Spaced Repetition**: Vocabulary reviewed at increasing intervals

## Detailed Grade 1-3 Curriculum

### Overview

```
Duration: 3 years (36 weeks/year)
Themes: 12 themes × 4 weeks = 48 weeks, staggered across 3 years
Content per Theme: 5 pieces (Vocabulary, Reading, Speaking, Writing, Listening)
Total Annual Content: 60 pieces/year = 180 pieces over 3 years
Assessment: Continuous (every session) + Summative (end of theme)
```

### UNIT 1: Personal Identity & Self-Expression (Weeks 1-4)

#### Theme 1.1: Who Am I?

**Duration:** 4 weeks (2 weeks intro + 2 weeks practice)

**Theme Objective:**
Students will be able to introduce themselves, state personal information, and describe basic feelings and preferences.

**Key Questions:**
- What is my name?
- How do I introduce myself?
- What do I like and dislike?
- How do I feel?

**Learning Outcomes:**

| Skill | A1 Beginner | Assessment Method |
|-------|------------|------------------|
| **Listening** | Can understand basic self-introduction phrases | MC comprehension Q&A |
| **Speaking** | Can say "My name is..." and basic preferences | Pronunciation assessment |
| **Reading** | Can identify personal words in simple text | Matching exercise |
| **Writing** | Can write "My name is..." with visual support | Sentence completion |

**Vocabulary Inventory (80 words)**

*Tier 1: High Frequency (Every student must know)*
```
Personal Information:
- name, age, boy, girl, student, person
- I, me, my, your
- am, is, are

Basic Verbs:
- like, dislike, love, hate
- have, has

Personal Descriptors:
- happy, sad, tired, angry, excited
- tall, short, smart, funny, kind, shy
```

*Tier 2: Supporting (Context-dependent)*
```
Family Connection Words:
- brother, sister, mother, father
- friend, teacher

Activity Words (in context):
- play, run, read, write, eat
- morning, afternoon, night
```

*Tier 3: Enrichment (Optional, for advanced)*
```
Emotions (extended):
- frustrated, proud, nervous, calm, peaceful
- amazed, surprised, confused

Character Traits (extended):
- curious, brave, honest, thoughtful
```

**Vocabulary Teaching Progression (Per Week)**

```
Week 1: Listening & Recognition Phase
├─ Daily: Phoneme recognition (sound discrimination)
├─ Model pronunciation (native speaker audio)
├─ Gesture/action association (TPR - Total Physical Response)
└─ Recognition exercises (pointing to pictures, yes/no responses)

Week 2: Speaking & Production Phase
├─ Guided repetition (choral, individual)
├─ Substitution drills (My name is _____)
├─ Peer practice (partner introductions)
└─ Production task (record self-introduction)

Week 3: Integration Phase
├─ Reading: Identify words in context
├─ Writing: Copy and complete sentences
├─ Listening: Comprehend in narrative
└─ Speaking: Use in dialogue practice

Week 4: Application & Assessment
├─ Independent use in various contexts
├─ Mini-project (create introduction poster)
└─ Assessment (individual introduction recording)
```

**Grammar Focus: Present Tense "To Be"**

```
SUBJECT PRONOUNS & "TO BE"
├─ I am...
├─ You are...
├─ He/She/It is...
└─ We/They are...

PRESENT SIMPLE FOR HABITS/FACTS
├─ I like (verb base form)
├─ I play (verb base form)
└─ I have (verb base form)

POSSESSIVE ADJECTIVES
├─ My name
├─ Your sister
├─ His/Her father
└─ Our friend

QUESTION FORMATION
├─ What is your name?
├─ How are you?
├─ Do you like...?
└─ What do you like?

NEGATIVE FORMATION
├─ I don't like...
├─ I'm not happy
└─ I don't have a...
```

## Learning Objectives Framework

### Bloom's Taxonomy Application for Grade 1-3

**Level 1: REMEMBER (Recognition & Recall)**
- Identify vocabulary items by picture
- Recognize familiar words in text
- Recall vocabulary meanings
- List family members
- Example Activity: Match word to picture, answer "What is...?"

**Level 2: UNDERSTAND (Comprehension)**
- Explain meaning of words in own words
- Describe pictures in simple terms
- Paraphrase simple sentences
- Summarize story in 1-2 sentences
- Example Activity: Answer "Who...?", "What...?", "Where...?"

**Level 3: APPLY (Using in New Context)**
- Use new vocabulary in sentences
- Apply grammar rules to new examples
- Use language in role play
- Complete cloze activities with appropriate words
- Example Activity: Fill in blanks, complete dialogues, use vocabulary in new contexts

**Level 4: ANALYZE (Breaking Down)**
- Distinguish between similar words
- Identify main idea vs. supporting details
- Compare two characters/stories
- Find key information in text
- Example Activity: Sequencing events, finding answers to "Who did what?"

**Level 5: EVALUATE (Making Judgments)**
- Decide if statements are true/false based on text
- Choose best answer from options
- Explain preferences (like/dislike)
- Judge appropriate behavior
- Example Activity: True/false, multiple choice with justification

**Level 6: CREATE (Making New Product)**
- Write own story or poem
- Create dialogue with partner
- Design poster about topic
- Make up own example
- Example Activity: Write "About Me" poster, create dialogue, tell own story

## Grammar Scope & Sequence

### A1 Beginner Grammar (Grade 1-3)

**Principle:** Grammar taught inductively through context, not rules

### Unit 1: Present Tense "To Be"

**Presentation:**
```
AFFIRMATIVE
I am happy
You are a student
He/She/It is 7 years old
We/They are friends

NEGATIVE
I am not sad
You are not tall
He/She/It is not big

QUESTIONS
Am I happy?
Are you a student?
Is he/she/it 7 years old?
Are we/they friends?
```

**Teaching Sequence:**
- Week 1: Introduce "I am" with gestures (Teacher models: "I am happy" + smile)
- Week 2: Add "You are" in dialogue (Teacher: "You are a student" pointing to student)
- Week 3: Introduce "He/She is" with third person (About character or classmate)
- Week 4: Expand to questions and negatives

**Practice Activities:**
- Choral repetition with gestures
- Substitution drills (I am ____, You are ____, etc.)
- Interview partner (Are you happy? Yes, I am)
- Mime and guess (Others guess: "You are tired" - yes!)

### Unit 2: Present Simple (Habitual Actions)

**Presentation:**
```
AFFIRMATIVE (Base form)
I like apples
You like to play
He/She likes milk
We/They like school

FREQUENCY
I always like apples
I sometimes eat fish
I never like vegetables

NEGATIVE
I don't like fish
You don't like sleep
He/She doesn't like...

QUESTIONS
Do you like apples?
Does he/she like milk?
What do you like?
```

**Teaching Sequence:**
- Week 1: Introduce with "like/don't like"
- Week 2: Add other verbs: play, eat, drink, read
- Week 3: Introduce frequency words (always, sometimes, never)
- Week 4: Practice questions and answers

### Unit 3: Possessives

**Presentation:**
```
POSSESSIVE ADJECTIVES
my name, your name, his name, her name, its name, 
our name, their name

POSSESSIVE PRONOUNS
mine, yours, his, hers, ours, theirs

CONTRACTIONS (Introduced later)
my = "My name is Alex"
your = "Your sister is nice"
```

**Teaching Sequence:**
- Week 1: Introduce possessives with family (my mother, your father)
- Week 2: Practice with classroom objects (my book, your pencil)
- Week 3: Use in dialogues ("Is this your book?")
- Week 4: Consolidate with writing activities

### Unit 4: Imperative (Commands)

**Presentation:**
```
AFFIRMATIVE COMMANDS
Wake up! Get dressed! Eat breakfast!
Go to school! Play with friends!

NEGATIVE COMMANDS
Don't sleep! Don't eat too much!
Don't be late!

POLITE REQUESTS
Please sit down. Can you help me?
Would you please listen?
```

**Teaching Sequence:**
- Week 1: Introduce simple commands (Wake up, sit down)
- Week 2: Use commands for classroom management (Listen! Stand up!)
- Week 3: Add "please" for politeness
- Week 4: Negative commands (Don't...)

### Unit 5: Question Formation

**Presentation:**
```
YES/NO QUESTIONS
Are you happy? - Yes, I am / No, I'm not
Do you like apples? - Yes, I do / No, I don't
Does he like apples? - Yes, he does / No, he doesn't

WH- QUESTIONS
What is your name? - My name is...
Where do you live? - I live in...
Who is your friend? - My friend is...
How old are you? - I'm 7 years old
How are you? - I'm fine, thank you

QUESTION PATTERNS
What + noun: What is your name?
What + verb: What do you like?
Who + verb: Who is your best friend?
```

**Teaching Sequence:**
- Week 1: Simple yes/no questions (Are you happy?)
- Week 2: What questions (What is your name?)
- Week 3: Who, Where, How questions
- Week 4: Practice in dialogues

### Grammar Teaching Principles for Grade 1-3

1. **Inductive Approach:** Present examples in context, let students discover pattern
2. **Comprehensible Input:** Vocabulary and grammar should be understandable from context
3. **Scaffolding:** Start simple, gradually add complexity
4. **Functional Grammar:** Teach grammar as tool for communication, not as rules to memorize
5. **Multiple Exposures:** Each grammar point taught across multiple units and weeks
6. **Meaning Before Form:** Focus on meaning first, form correction later
7. **No Metalanguage:** Don't teach grammar terminology (don't say "present simple")
8. **Visual Support:** Use gestures, pictures, actions to show grammar meaning

- 4-6 lines of dialogue per conversation
